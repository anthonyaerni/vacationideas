The snow outlook

You asked about El Niño — and it turns out that's exactly what's forming, not what we assumed going in. NOAA's Climate Prediction Center has a strong-to-record-strength El Niño Advisory in effect as of Aug 13, 2026, with a >90% chance of a very strong event and roughly 69% odds it becomes the strongest on record. That's a sharp flip from last winter's weak La Niña, which produced Colorado's record-low April snowpack.

Here's the nuance that actually matters for your trip: El Niño classically favors southern Colorado, but strong events also drive a southerly storm track that induces easterly upslope winds — and upslope storms load up the terrain near/east of the Continental Divide regardless of the north/south split. That's exactly where Bear Lake sits. OpenSnow's Aug 11, 2026 seasonal outlook says it directly: "Front Range foothills and cities often tend to see above-average snowfall during strong/super El Niño's." Summit County gets no such tailwind — OpenSnow's own words: "Keystone, Breck, Copper, Vail, Aspen, Crested Butte, and Steamboat do not have a clear signal in either direction."

One more wrinkle for your specific dates: OpenSnow's seasonality signal favors Oct–Dec and Mar–Apr, with a below-average signal for Jan–Feb. Your window straddles that seam — the days through New Year's Eve sit in the more favorable half, and the first few days of January sit right at the edge of the softer patch. Nothing to panic about four-plus months out, just don't bank on it.

Photography & snowshoeing

Rocky Mountain National Park

Trail Ridge Road is closed the whole trip (it shuts mid-October, reopens Memorial Day) — everything below is reachable via Bear Lake Road, Wild Basin, or Lily Lake instead. No timed-entry reservation is needed in this window either; that system only runs May 22–Oct 18. You just need the standard $30 (1-day) or $35 (7-day) entrance pass.

Alphy can't come on any hike — worth flagging even though he's with you for most of the trip. RMNP's pet policy is strict and binary: dogs are allowed on a 6-foot leash in parking lots, on paved roads, and in campgrounds — and nowhere else. Not the Bear Lake loop, not Sprague Lake, nothing. On actual hiking/snowshoeing days that means leaving him back at the house, trading off with each other in the car, or asking a friend to watch him for a few hours — boarding in Estes Park (Animal Hospital of the Rockies, Estes Park Pet Lodge, Elena's Barking Lot, roughly $40–80/night) is there as a backup if you ever want it, just not something we're budgeting for by default. He's welcome for every driving/wildlife-viewing day below.
Sprague Lake

~0.9mi flat loop. Frozen lake with a near-perfect Continental Divide reflection — the easiest great sunrise shot on this list.

Bear Lake

~0.6mi loop. Iconic Hallett Peak/Flattop backdrop right from the trailhead. Lot fills before sunrise on busy days — get there early.

Nymph → Dream → Emerald Lake

~3.6mi RT, ~650ft gain. Three progressively better frozen alpine lakes and heavy snow-ghost trees — the classic RMNP winter postcard. Bring snowshoes for above Dream Lake.

Cub Lake

~4.6mi RT. Beaver ponds and an aspen grove, quieter than the Bear Lake corridor. Snowshoes likely for the last mile.

Lily Lake

Short, flat, roadside. Longs Peak and Mt. Meeker catch first light directly across the lake — best low-effort sunrise spot on the list.

Wild Basin

Road gate closes ~1mi before the summer trailhead in winter, adding ~2mi RT. Partially frozen waterfalls, quieter, less broken-in trail — snowshoes recommended.

Wildlife: Moraine Park and Horseshoe Park are the best December bets for elk at dawn/dusk; Horseshoe Park/Sheep Lakes for bighorn sheep (rutting season runs into December). All roadside pullouts — the one place Alphy can actually join the sightseeing.

Cold-weather camera notes for the 60D and GoPro: cold can roughly halve battery life, so carry spares in an inner jacket pocket and rotate them — a "dead" battery often revives once rewarmed. Never charge a lithium battery below freezing. Bring the camera into a warm car sealed in a zip-top bag first, or you'll fog the sensor with condensation. Turn off GoPro Wi-Fi/GPS to save power. Around the solstice, expect sunrise ~7:20am and sunset ~4:35pm — plan to be in position, not mid-hike, at those times.

Snowshoes vs. microspikes: you've already got both, so just bring them along — which one a given trail wants is genuinely storm-dependent. Lower/south-facing/packed trails (Bear Lake, Lily Lake) usually only need microspikes, while shaded/higher trails (Emerald Lake, Wild Basin, the last mile to Cub Lake) more often need snowshoes after a fresh storm.

Getting Serra kitted out

Gear

Anthony's already AIARE 1-certified and already has his own backcountry gear, and Serra's beacon is sorted — so this is short. No course needed either; Anthony's teaching her himself. What's actually left to buy is skis, bindings, a shovel, a probe, skins, and poles.

What's left to buy

    Consider renting a full AT package from Bentgate in Golden first ($75–110/day, cheaper after 2 days) for her first several tours before buying skis outright — confirms fit and preference before committing.

    Teaching her yourself

    1 · Mechanics on easy terrain

    Skinning, kick turns, and transitions first, somewhere mellow and low-stakes — before any real avalanche terrain is anywhere nearby.

    2 · Practice terrain

    Uphill passes at Eldora (free for passholders) or A-Basin ($119/season, $39 add-on) let her drill transitions on patrolled, avalanche-controlled terrain — zero real exposure, cheap repetition before a real tour.

    3 · First real tour

    Berthoud Pass's "Old Terrain Park" sits around 25° — below the ~26° threshold typically needed to slide — and is heavily skier-compacted. Genuinely one of the safer places in Colorado for her first real backcountry terrain, with Anthony's own AIARE training doing the route-finding.

    Since it's just the two of you without a course structure enforcing it: still check CAIC's daily forecast before every single outing, no exceptions, even at "safer" zones like Berthoud's Old Terrain Park, and run a quick beacon-search/companion-rescue drill together before her first real tour.
    20–30 minutes from Golden

    Boulder & Denver

    Both cities' holiday lights run the length of your whole window, so there's no real scheduling pressure — pick whichever plan option puts them where you want a change of pace.

    Chautauqua Park, Boulder

    Leashed dogs welcome on the lawn and most trails at the base of the Flatirons — Alphy's best outing of the city days. Go at golden hour for the classic photo.

    Pearl Street Mall, Boulder

    Lights run late Nov through January, plus the Dushanbe Teahouse's ~275,000-light display next door. Dogs aren't allowed on the Mall itself, even leashed — walk Alphy at Chautauqua or the Creek Path instead, then do the Mall without him.

    Celestial Seasonings tour, Boulder

    $6pp, 45-min factory tour, unlimited tea samples after — a genuinely distinctive, low-key Boulder-only stop. Tue–Sat, 10am–5pm.

    Civic Center Park & 16th Street, Denver

    Nightly light-and-music show on the City & County Building at 5:45 and 6:45pm, plus the 100ft Mile High Tree on 16th Street — both free and run the whole season.

    Larimer Square & Union Station, Denver

    Historic block with a nightly projection light show, plus Union Station's 40ft tree next door — an easy walkable evening, good paired with dinner at Milk Market.

    McGregor Square ice rink, Denver

    Outdoor rink near Coors Field, roughly $6 to skate — the real ice-skating option this season since downtown Denver's free rink is closed for park reconstruction and Boulder's outdoor rink has permanently closed.

    Blossoms of Light, Denver Botanic Gardens

    Runs Nov 20, 2026–Jan 10, 2027 (closed Dec 25), 4:30–9pm — book timed tickets ahead, popular nights sell out. Dogs not allowed (service animals only).

    Washington Park / City Park, Denver

    Both allow leashed dogs and make for a good flat winter walk if you want Alphy along for a Denver morning before the ticketed stuff.

    For dinner: Frasca Food and Wine or The Kitchen in Boulder; Tavernetta or Guard and Grace in Denver — book ahead for holiday week either way. Meow Wolf's Convergence Station or the Stranahan's distillery tour are good indoor backups if a day turns cold or snowy.

    Passes & driving

    Ski days & Breckenridge

    No single pass covers both halves of this trip — Breckenridge is Vail Resorts/Epic-exclusive, and every close-to-Golden option (Eldora, Winter Park, A-Basin, Copper) is Ikon-exclusive. A hybrid beats committing to either pass in full.

    Recommended: a 3-day Epic Day Pass at the 32-Resort restricted tier (covers Breckenridge and Keystone, skip the pricier Vail/Beaver Creek tier you won't use) for the Breck-based days, plus single-day advance tickets at Loveland and Eldora for the close-to-home days. That beats either a full Ikon Session Pass alone or an all-resort Epic Day Pass you'd only partly use.
