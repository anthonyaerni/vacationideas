Christmas & New Year's

Dec 24 is the family night everywhere — expect shorter hours, not closed doors, in tourist towns.

Nochebuena (Dec 24) empties the streets as families head to midnight Mass and tamale dinners; tourist-town restaurants stay open but on lighter staffing. Dec 25 and Jan 1 are quiet in the way a country's own holiday is quiet — banks and government close, hotels and beach bars don't. San José's Zapote fair and the Tope horse parade run this same week if you have airport layover hours to spend.

New Year's Eve in Tamarindo is one of the country's best-known beach parties — bonfires, beachfront DJs, and fireworks up and down the shore from Langosta to Playa Grande at midnight. Everything worth doing is walkable; don't plan on driving anywhere that night. Book lodging early — prime spots go 6–9 months out.

Activities

Honest read: strong on hiking, biking, kayaking and surf. Thin on rock climbing and art — those are half-day add-ons here, not pillars.
Hiking

Cerro Chato's ~5hr round trip to a jade crater lake is the marquee day, paired with La Fortuna Waterfall's 530-step descent.

Rincón de la Vieja adds dry-forest trails to bubbling mud pots and a hot-spring soak — a good day trip from Tamarindo.

Rock climbing

This isn't a climbing country — the real crags (Cachí, Provi­dencia) sit near San José, off-route. From Tamarindo, Playa Flamingo is a legit seaside sport crag (5.7–5.13) bookable through TamaRocks. Treat it as a fun half-day, not a trip pillar.

Kayaking / canoeing

The Tamarindo Estuary mangrove paddle — crocodiles, herons, howler monkeys — is beginner-friendly and needs zero experience.

Mountain biking

Desafío and Bike Arenal run ~6hr guided rides around Lake Arenal through farmland, jungle, and river crossings, volcano always in view.

Surfing

Tamarindo's main beach is genuinely forgiving for a beginner; Playa Grande, 10 minutes off, is a real step up in power — warm water (~79°F) year-round.

Food

Casado plates and gallo pinto are the daily rhythm; ceviche is a genuine strength on the coast. Nothing wild by global standards, but ingredient-driven and fruit-heavy — La Fortuna's mid-range dining scene outperforms expectations.

Art / museums

Not a museum country, and none of these three towns have more than gift-shop galleries. San José's Jade and Gold Museums are worth a layover if you have one — don't build the trip around this.

Bikepacking — not realistic here

La Fortuna to Monteverde is 15km as the crow flies but 3–4 hours to drive — narrow, mountainous, unpaved in stretches, shared with fast tour vans. Point-to-point touring isn't a good idea on this route. The guided MTB days above are the honest substitute.

Daylight is short near the solstice — sunrise ~7:45am, sunset ~5:16pm in Lisbon. Plan outdoor days to start early and wrap by 5pm.

Christmas & New Year's

Lisbon and Seville both go big on lights this time of year — but real closures on Dec 25 and Jan 1 in both countries need planning around.

Rua Augusta and Baixa string up lights, a 30m tree rises over Praça do Comércio, and the Rossio Christmas Market runs mulled wine and cork-craft stalls through the season. Seville matches it stride for stride — over 300 streets and squares lit through early January, with Calle Tetuán and the façades around Plaza San Francisco the standout stroll, plus a traditional nativity-scene market (Feria de Belenes) near the cathedral. Most shops, monuments, and family restaurants close on Christmas Day and New Year's Day in both countries — the Alhámbra, Real Alcázar, and both cathedrals included — so book a hotel with its own restaurant or treat those two days as travel days.

New Year's Eve at Praça do Comércio is the trip's finale — DJs and live acts from ~9:30pm, then a 10-minute fireworks show synced with 250 drones over the Tagus at midnight, 100,000+ people in the square. Arrive by 9–10pm, or book a rooftop bar (Topo Chiado, Memmo Alfama) for a calmer, paid view.

Activities

Honest read: this is a food-and-culture trip first, with enough hiking, climbing, kayaking and surf on the side to keep it from feeling sleepy. Nothing here is a multi-day commitment.
Art & culture — the trip's real strength

Lisbon's Gulbenkian (5,000 years of collection, Rembrandt to Monet), MAAT's riverside architecture, and the National Tile Museum. Seville's Real Alcázar (€21pp) and Cathedral/Giralda (€14pp), then Granada's Alhambra — book the moment your dates are fixed, tickets release 90 days out and holiday week adds real risk of selling out.

Add a flamenco show in Seville's Triana or Santa Cruz (Los Gallos, Casa de la Memoria — roughly €28–38pp with a drink) for one proper night out.

Food

18 Michelin-starred restaurants in Lisbon alone, plus Manteigaria's pas­téis de nata. Seville's tapas circuit centers on jamón ibérico, salmorejo and sherry at century-old bars like El Rinconcillo. Granada flips the model entirely — order a drink, get a free tapa, repeat down Calle Navas.

Hiking

Sintra's forested hills and the Seven Hanging Valleys Trail near Lagos, plus a genuinely easy day-walk near Granada through the Alpujarras' white villages (Bubión–Pampaneira–Capileira) along old Moorish irrigation channels — scenic, shaded, no technical ground.

Rock climbing

Sintra (Farol da Guia, ~80 routes) and south-facing Arrábida cover the Portugal side. If you want one bigger day out, El Chorro/Caminito del Rey near Málaga is a real crag and a spectacular cliffside walkway — but it's a full ~7hr day from Seville or Granada, so treat it as optional, not a must-do.

Kayaking

Benagil Cave, guided kayak or boat only, runs year-round — mornings are calmer and far less crowded than summer. Book flexible/refundable given winter swell risk.

Surfing

Ericeira and Sagres/Arrifana get powerful December groundswell — genuinely expert-level on the reef breaks. Sheltered beaches (Foz do Lizandro, Zavial) work fine for a lesson. 4/3mm wetsuit minimum.

A day on two wheels, if you want it

No dedicated cycling leg here — just a casual half-day option, like an easy coastal ride around the Algarve or renting bikes for an afternoon in Seville's old town. Nice to have, not a plan to build the trip around.

Getting between the two countries

Keep the same rental car for the whole loop rather than a one-way drop — crossing the border and back is a small fee; a one-way drop between Spain and Portugal can run €500+.

Drive Faro → Seville yourselves (~125mi, ~2–2.5hr, no tolls) — the most realistic option since the car's already with you from the Algarve. Park it near the edge of Seville's old town (the historic core is narrow and largely pedestrianized) and get around on foot. For Granada, leave the car parked in Seville and take the train (~2.5–3.5hr, ~$35–40pp), then train back to Seville to reclaim the car and drive back through the Algarve toward Lisbon for New Year's.

Christmas & New Year's

This is Kiwis' own summer holiday week too — not just international tourist peak. Book everything now, this far out is already late for hut bookings.

Christmas Day itself runs quieter than you'd expect in small NZ towns — family holiday, reduced hours, book dinner ahead or plan a low-key trail day. Multi-day Great Walk hut bookings (Routeburn, Kepler) for this window opened back in May 2026 and sell out within minutes — by now, plan on day-hiking the highlights instead of overnighting them.

Queenstown NYE centers on a free public fireworks show at Earnslaw Park over Lake Wakatipu, entertainment from ~7:30pm and fireworks at midnight, with Steamer Wharf running its own lakefront party alongside. Expect the whole town genuinely packed — this is the loudest, busiest week of the New Zealand year, on top of everything else.

Activities

Honest read: this is the strongest activity roster of the three by a wide margin — and the weakest on surf and on ease of getting there.
Hiking

Great Walk huts (Routeburn, Kepler, Abel Tasman Coast Track) are booked out by now for this window — but Routeburn Flats/Falls and Roys Peak/Isthmus Peak in Wanaka are full-day classics with no booking required.

Rock climbing

Hospital Flat, 20 minutes from Wanaka, is the country's largest sport-climbing area — 800+ routes, sun-drenched, dries fast. Wanaka Mountain Guides runs guided days.

Kayaking — the icon

Abel Tasman sea kayaking among seal colonies and golden beaches is the single most iconic paddle in the country. Guided multi-day lodge trips run $400–1,200pp; self-guided freedom rentals are far cheaper.

Mountain biking

Queenstown Bike Park off the Skyline Gondola — 28+ gravity trails from beginner to expert across a 30km+ network, genuinely world-class, not a token activity.

Surfing — the weak link

Queenstown/Wanaka/Abel Tasman are landlocked or calm-water. Dunedin's St Clair is the real South Island break, but its best season is autumn/winter, not December. If surf is a priority, this isn't the trip for it.

Food & wine

Central Otago wine country (Gibbston Valley, Bannockburn) for full-day tastings; Fergburger and a strong small-city dining scene in Queenstown; Rippon Vineyard's lake view in Wanaka.

Art / museums

Dunedin's Toitū Otago Settlers Museum (free, 100,000+ objects) is the honest pick — a manageable ~4hr drive. Wellington's Te Papa is genuinely excellent but requires a flight-plus-ferry detour that doesn't fit this route.

Bikepacking — a taste, not the whole trail

The Otago Central Rail Trail (152km, Clyde to Middlemarch) is one of NZ's most famous cycle-touring routes, but the full 3–5 day version doesn't realistically fit alongside everything else in 13–15 days. The honest compromise: a half-day e-bike ride (Clyde → Alexandra and back) slotted into the Central Otago wine day.

Honorable mentions

Didn't make the final three

Worth a look if none of the above lands, or for a future trip.

Nicaragua · San Juan del Sur

Costa Rica's cheaper, rowdier cousin — same surf and volcano hiking, lower cost, rougher infrastructure.

Panama · Bocas del Toro / Boquete

Surf plus cloud-forest hiking and coffee country, cheaper than Costa Rica, less polished tourist infrastructure.

Thailand · Krabi / Railay

World-class rock climbing over turquoise water and incredible, very affordable food — against a brutal ~24hr flight.

Guatemala · Lake Atiitlán / Antigua

Volcano hiking, kayaking, colonial art and markets, very affordable — thin on surf and mountain biking.

Puerto Rico

No passport, short flight, surf and rainforest hiking — limited real rock climbing or mountain biking, pricier than Central America.

Chile · Patagonia / Santiago

Southern Hemisphere summer, world-class hiking, climbing and wine — same long-flight, high-cost tradeoff as New Zealand.

Morocco · Marrakech / Atlas Mountains

Incredible food, art and markets, affordable, real High Atlas hiking — no real surf or kayaking fit.

Hawaii · Big Island / Kauaʻi

No passport, ~6hr flight, warm surf and hiking — pricier lodging, weak on rock climbing and art.
